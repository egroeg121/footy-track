# Exploiting video / temporal structure (fixed-centre PTZ) for broadcast football camera calibration

Scope note: researched 2026-11-14. Sources were fetched and read in full text where marked "(full text read)"; everything else is from abstracts / search snippets and is flagged. Several 2022-2026 items are cited only from abstracts because the PDFs were not readable.

## 1. PTZ camera models for sports: how do they get the fixed camera centre and per-frame PTZ, and how accurate are they?

### Takeaway
The standard model (Chen, Zhu & Little) splits P = K Qφ Qθ S[I|-C] into a time-invariant "base" (centre C, base rotation S, estimated once per stadium/camera) and a per-frame (pan, tilt, f) triple; with the base known, 2 correspondences suffice per frame. The newest evidence (BroadTrack, WACV 2025) says a literal fixed optical centre is too strict for 25x broadcast lenses (nodal point moves with zoom, and lens distortion matters most), but a soft "tripod" constraint (fixed rotation centre T, optical-axis point at fixed distance δ) gives physically plausible, smooth tracks and the best published numbers on SoccerNet-GSR.

### Cited Findings
- Two-point PTZ model: P = K Qφ Qθ S[I|-C]; S[I|-C] is time-invariant and can be estimated from images at various PTZ settings and then used as a prior; f is the only intrinsic unknown (square pixels, principal point at image centre, no distortion). Two ray/pixel pairs give pan, tilt, f; one RANSAC min-sample of 2 needs 16 iterations vs 71 for 4-point homography (99% success, 50% outliers) — [Chen, Zhu, Little, "A Two-point Method for PTZ Camera Calibration in Sports" (full text read)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.09005); code at https://github.com/lood339/two_point_calib
- Two-point method base estimation: ground-truth cameras were built by calibrating images with >=4 correspondences, running a global optimisation to estimate the PTZ base, then calibrating the remaining images with the two-point method, iterating until good. On two World Cup 2014 games they found "the assumption of fixed PTZ camera base generally holds in soccer broadcasting" — [same paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.09005)
- Two-point accuracy (single-image, pan-tilt random forest + SIFT): mean IoU 0.83 vs CalibMe 0.68 on a highlights dataset (4 sequences, 1280x720); runtime 0.3 s/frame vs 3.0 s (SIFT ~0.2 s + prediction/optimisation ~0.1 s, C++ on 3 GHz Mac CPU, unoptimised); >85% of frames under 1 deg rotation error; failures concentrate at FOV < ~25 deg; model has no lens distortion — [same paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.09005)
- Lu, Chen & Little "Pan-tilt-zoom SLAM for Sports Videos" (BMVC 2019, full text read): extends the two-point model to sequences. Inputs: camera location, tripod base rotation and first-frame pose are assumed known. State = [pan, tilt, f, and their velocities] tracked with an EKF; landmarks are *rays* (pan, tilt angles in tripod frame) so no depth is needed; keyframes go into an online "pan-tilt random forest" (SIFT descriptor -> ray) used for relocalisation (ray angular tolerance 0.1 deg when deciding to update vs add a tree); players removed with Faster R-CNN person boxes; RANSAC + two-point init in relocalisation — [arXiv 1907.08816](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.08816); code https://github.com/lulufa390/Pan-tilt-zoom-SLAM
- PTZ-SLAM numbers: real-data mean abs error vs human annotation (GT itself ~1.5 px basketball / ~2 px soccer reprojection error). Soccer (333 frames, 720p30, f changes 4000->2000 px in ~2 s): pan 0.08 deg, tilt 0.08 deg, f 63.7 px for PTZ-SLAM vs 0.74 deg / 0.13 deg / 179 px for a homography-EKF baseline ("EKF-H", online version of Lisanti et al.); no-player-detection ablation: pan 0.28, f 110.6 px. Basketball (3600 frames): pan 0.17 deg, tilt 0.05, f 19.3 px vs EKF-H 3.63 deg / 0.34 / 395 px. An offline keyframe-matching baseline had lower mean error on soccer (pan 0.04) but on basketball max pan error 15.89 deg vs 0.96 deg for PTZ-SLAM (no temporal info). Synthetic: relocalisation correct (<2 deg) 100% vs 67.7% for nearest-keyframe at 40% outliers — [arXiv 1907.08816](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.08816)
- Independent replication problem: BroadTrack authors report PTZ-SLAM's released code "crashes after processing about 200 frames" on SoccerNet-gamestate (completeness 26.67%, JaC5 25.87, MRE 27.64 px) — [BroadTrack (full text read)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721)
- BroadTrack (Magera, Hoyoux, Barnich, Van Droogenbroeck; EVS Broadcast / ULiege; WACV 2025): camera = f, radial k1 (Brown-Conrady, normalised coords), pan/tilt/roll, centre C; principal point at image centre, square pixels. Tripod model: pan and tilt axes intersect at a fixed point T for the whole game; a point O on the optical axis stays at fixed distance δ from T; focal point C = T - δ r2 + λ r3, with {T, δ} fixed and λ varying per frame (so the optical centre *moves along the axis with zoom*). Authors explicitly argue the focal point "may not be located inside the camera and … should evolve along the optical axis as the camera zooms" — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721)
- BroadTrack estimates T: run the tracker *without* the tripod term, then optimise T and δ over the estimated cameras; before that, a default focal-point prior is used (main camera (0, 55, -12) m, 16 m cameras (±36, 55, -12), high-behind-goal (-65, 0, -15) in the sn-calibration frame; (0, 90, -18) for a Bundesliga stadium); for the 20-min Bundesliga clip T/δ were fitted on the first 5000 frames — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721); repo with `compute_tripod.py`: https://github.com/evs-broadcast/BroadTrack (C++/CMake, Docker, GPU)
- BroadTrack results on SoccerNet-gamestate test (49 x 750 frames, HD): JaC@5px 56.88, JaC@10px 79.79, MRE 5.02 px, 100% completeness; vs NBJW 37.14 / 68.24 / 10.28 px / 93.67%; TVCalib 19.88 / 50.42 / 12.4 / 99.93; "Ours (fixed C)" i.e. forcing a fixed focal point 50.97 / 74.93 / 5.39 — i.e. fixed optical centre hurts. Runs at 16 fps on HD with 2x RTX 4090, unoptimised — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721)
- BroadTrack ablation (JaC5 / JaC10 / MRE / MedRE): baseline (no OF, no tripod, no k1) 42.09 / 74.09 / 5.74 / 3.13; +k1 54.10 / 78.35 / 5.04 / 2.47; +k1+OF 55.96 / 79.07 / 4.85 / 2.43; +k1+tripod 54.9 / 78.99 / 4.95 / 2.44; all three 56.88 / 79.79 / 5.02 / 2.37. Radial distortion is by far the largest single gain; flow and tripod add little to single-frame metrics but matter for smoothness: NBJW's per-frame focal point "can travel up to 20 meters along a single sequence" while BroadTrack's stays near T; flow smooths focal length. Residual high-frequency focal-length jitter is attributed to a "vertigo" ambiguity (small camera-position error compensated by f) — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721)
- Long-term: 20 min (60,000 frames) of Bundesliga main-camera footage, only 60 reinitialisations (avg 4 frames each), overlay "almost perfect" (qualitative only, commercial keypoint/line detector) — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721)
- Puwein, Ziegler, Ballan, Pollefeys, "PTZ camera network calibration from moving people in sports broadcasts" (WACV 2012): self-calibration of rotating/zooming cameras + foot trajectories of moving people for wide-baseline multi-camera registration, ground-plane lines to refine, final bundle adjustment of all extrinsics/intrinsics. Only abstract-level info retrieved; no accuracy numbers obtained — [mlanthology listing](https://mlanthology.org/wacv/2012/puwein2012wacv-ptz); [Possegger et al. description](https://www.tugraz.at/fileadmin/user_upload/Institute/ICG/Documents/lrs/pubs/possegger_cvww_2012.pdf)
- Classical PTZ self-calibration: Agapito, Hayman, Reid (IJCV 2001): fixed-location rotating/zooming camera; inter-image homographies are the infinite homography; linear (zero skew etc.) and ML estimators; radial distortion is a significant error source — [abstract/index](https://typeset.io/pdf/self-calibration-of-rotating-and-zooming-cameras-3b5cx1i1c3.pdf) (abstract level)
- Sinha & Pollefeys (CVIU 2006): calibrate lowest-zoom intrinsics + radial distortion from an extended panorama via bundle adjustment (sub-pixel alignment), then intrinsics and *zoom-dependent* radial distortion over the full zoom range with a "Zoom Bundle"; mean reprojection error within 0.43 px for 35-40 images; notes inherent ambiguity between radial distortion at one zoom and compensation at another — [Sinha CVIU 2006 PDF (full text read)](https://snsinha.github.io/pdfs/SinhaCVIU2006.pdf)
- PTZ-Calib (Guo et al., Feb 2025, Alibaba): offline stage = uniformly chosen overlapping reference images covering 360 deg, *PTZ Incremental Bundle Adjustment* (ray landmarks, global BA to curb drift), optional alignment to geographic frame; online stage = relocalisation of any new viewpoint against the references. Model assumes no offset between projection centre and rotation centre (offset listed as future work). Timing: offline 29.8 s total (11.9 s non-linear optimisation, 8.8 s matching), online 0.68 s/frame. WorldCup IoU_part mean 96.8 / IoU_whole 93.1 (KpSFR 96.7 / 92.5, TVCalib 95.8 / 90.9) — [PTZ-Calib (full text read)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09075); code https://github.com/gjgjh/PTZ-Calib
- WorldPose (ETH/FIFA, ECCV 2024) broadcast cameras: per-frame focal length, principal point, 3 radial coefficients and rotation, with camera location held constant per clip; initialised with a commercial semi-automatic tool (Viz Arena, normally needs a pre-game scan), refined with Geman-McClure field-marking + player-keypoint reprojection, plus a smoothness regulariser on frame-to-frame change of f and distortion and a Lucas-Kanade flow-consistency term on field-marking points; no standalone pixel-level calibration error reported (evidence is qualitative; ~8 cm end-to-end pose error vs Vicon) — [WorldPose HTML + supplement](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02771v2)
- Citraro et al. (MVA 2020): per-frame keypoint network + condensation particle filter (300 particles) on pose and moving-median filter on focal length; *no* fixed-centre constraint; removing the particle filter raises mean angular error 1.681 -> 6.962 deg on basketball; WC14 single-image IoU 0.939; 8-32 fps on a TITAN X — [ar5iv 2003.14109](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.14109)

### Inferences
- For this project the strongest-evidenced recipe is BroadTrack-style: soft tripod constraint (T fixed per camera/stadium, f and k1 per frame), not a hard fixed centre; a hard fixed C costs ~6 JaC5 points on SoccerNet-GSR.
- The per-stadium prior (T, δ, k1) is a one-off estimation per match/camera that can be done on a few hundred well-calibrated frames (BroadTrack used 5000 frames) and then frozen, which is the main way a few good frames calibrate many.
- Lens distortion (k1) is worth more than any temporal trick on the 5 px JaC metric; homography-only registration cannot represent it.

### Gaps
- No accuracy numbers retrieved for Puwein et al. 2012 (abstract only).
- No peer-reviewed measurement of how far the real nodal point moves with zoom on a 25x lens; BroadTrack's claim is argued/indirect (fixed-C ablation), not measured with ground truth.
- "Chen et al. on PTZ calibration" beyond the 2018 two-point paper and Chen & Little's synthetic-data paper (arXiv 1810.10658, not read) were not examined in detail.

## 2. Frame-to-frame homography / feature propagation, drift, and the pure-rotation constraint

### Takeaway
Chaining frame-to-frame homographies or KLT tracks with no re-anchoring drifts badly on football (about 35% IoU with KLT, ~70% with a SIFT variant, vs 85% for per-frame registration in Sharma et al.), and sparse/dense flow must exclude players (ECC homography without masking is the worst tracker tested in a 2026 ablation). Working systems anchor to the field model every frame (line/keypoint reprojection) and use flow only to bridge frames with few markings, gated by a line-overlap confidence with automatic re-initialisation.

### Cited Findings
- Pure-rotation constraint: for a camera that only rotates about its centre, image pairs are related by H = K2 R K1^-1 (the infinite homography); Agapito et al. build self-calibration on this — [Agapito/Hayman/Reid abstract](https://typeset.io/pdf/self-calibration-of-rotating-and-zooming-cameras-3b5cx1i1c3.pdf). BroadTrack notes that frame-to-frame homographies assume pure rotation "an assumption that other works refute for soccer", although a homography between frames restricted to *field-plane* points is valid regardless — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721)
- Chaining without correction: Sharma et al. (WACV 2018, full text read) report on two long football sequences (200 labelled frames each at 2/s): KLT-based tracking (Okuma-style) 35% mean IoU ("drift and lack of features"), SIFT-based variant 70%, per-frame dictionary registration 85%, + MRF temporal smoothing 87%; manual re-initialisation needed ~400 times per 45-min video because of shot changes — [arXiv 1703.01437](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.01437)
- Hybrid anchoring (hockey): Gupta, Little et al. keep keyframes with known homographies (5 manually chosen), compute H_lin from line/ellipse features and H_tr = H_prev x frame-to-frame H from SIFT matches, pick the one with the lower residual-area error as initialisation and then minimise a geometric (area) error — explicitly to avoid drift accumulation — [Gupta et al. CRV 2011 (full text read)](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/lci/thesis/ankgupta/gupta11crv.pdf)
- BroadTrack per-frame update: start from previous camera κ_{t-1}; minimise L = L_F (field-marking points from semantic segmentation + mean-shift vs nearest projected marking, Cauchy loss) + L_OF + ω L_T (tripod). Optical flow term: pyramidal Lucas-Kanade matches kept only inside the projected side-line polygon, discarded around RTMDet player boxes, lifted to the Z=0 plane with the previous camera and reprojected with the new one (Cauchy loss). Flow is explicitly to "prevent drifting" where markings are sparse. Levenberg-Marquardt solver — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721)
- BroadTrack confidence/re-anchoring: score s = Jaccard index between the segmentation-derived marking mask and the projected template; s < 0.5 triggers re-initialisation (and flow is switched off to avoid contaminating the solve). Re-init = 2 keypoint correspondences -> f (Gedikli closed form), then iterated pan/tilt update, focal point set to tripod centre, roll 0. On 49 test sequences re-init was used 33 times, ~15 frames of lost tracking each — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721)
- Tracker ablation for camera propagation (SMART, 2026, WorldPose val 19 clips, rotation error vs GT): ECC homography 0.107 deg (worst; "no mechanism to ignore" players, systematic drift), sparse Lucas-Kanade+RANSAC 0.043, RAFT-small 0.043, RAFT-small + MAD 3-sigma outlier filter 0.041 (55 ms/frame), RAFT-large + MAD 0.041 (110 ms/frame, no gain). Inter-frame homography via RANSAC then decomposed to (R_t, t_t); updates with |ΔR| > 60 deg rejected; dense flow sampled at stride 4 inside the landmark convex hull. Pure frame-to-frame propagation from a known initial pose with no re-anchoring (so errors are cumulative within a clip). Hardware for the ms numbers is not stated — [SMART, arXiv 2605.31551 (full text read)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.31551)
- Lu et al. show a homography-parameterised EKF (EKF-H) accumulates more error than the (pan, tilt, f) parameterisation: synthetic reprojection error mean 2.1 px vs 0.7 px (seq 4, 0.08 deg/s), 1.0/16.0 max vs 0.3/0.5 (seq 3); fewer DOF = robust to outliers — [arXiv 1907.08816](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.08816)
- Player masking is worth it: removing the detector from PTZ-SLAM raises soccer focal-length error from 63.7 px to 110.6 px and pan from 0.08 to 0.28 deg — [arXiv 1907.08816](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.08816)
- Per-frame deep approach with filter-based temporal fusion: Citraro particle filter described above (re-init when per-frame pose has more inliers than filtered pose) — [ar5iv 2003.14109](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.14109)

### Inferences
- A practical loop for a fixed-centre PTZ: (1) calibrate "anchor" frames from a per-frame detector (keypoints/lines); (2) between anchors, estimate (Δpan, Δtilt, Δf) by flow on masked background, using the pure-rotation model (a 3-parameter model rather than 8-DOF homography) — this is what Lu et al. and BroadTrack effectively do; (3) at every frame add a line-reprojection term whenever markings are visible; (4) monitor a line-overlap IoU and re-anchor when it falls below ~0.5.
- Mask players (detector boxes or MAD outlier rejection on flow residuals); unmasked ECC is a trap for football.
- Background features off the pitch plane (stands, boards) are valid for the pure-rotation model only if the camera centre is truly fixed; BroadTrack restricts flow to the field plane, which avoids that assumption.

### Gaps
- No source found quantifying drift per second/frame for chained homographies on modern broadcast footage; the numbers above are task-level IoU or rotation error vs ground truth.
- SMART's flow is not combined with line refinement, so it does not show what line re-anchoring adds on top.
- OpenCV `findTransformECC` documentation and CPU timings could not be retrieved (docs.opencv.org returned 403).

## 3. Joint estimation (bundle adjustment) across a match: shared centre/intrinsics + per-frame rotation/zoom

### Takeaway
Nothing in the literature found reports a full-match joint optimisation with measured gain over per-frame estimation; the closest evidence is (a) panorama/zoom bundle adjustment for PTZ cameras (sub-pixel, 0.43 px mean reprojection), (b) PTZ-Calib's incremental + global BA with ray landmarks, and (c) BroadTrack's two-stage "track without tripod, then fit T and δ" which is a lightweight version of the same idea and mainly buys physical consistency rather than higher per-frame scores.

### Cited Findings
- Sinha & Pollefeys: Bundle I/II (shared radial distortion + intrinsics at a zoom level, per-image rotation) and Zoom Bundle (common params across zoom levels, per-view R_i, K_i) -> sub-pixel panorama alignment; mean reprojection error within 0.43 px over 35-40 images (200-300 matches/pair) — [Sinha CVIU 2006](https://snsinha.github.io/pdfs/SinhaCVIU2006.pdf)
- PTZ-Calib: incremental BA adds frames one by one (ray landmarks triangulated by averaging, first frame defines centre = 0), then global BA over all registered images + ray landmarks "to mitigate drift errors and maintain global consistency"; local-coordinate result optionally aligned to world frame by extra 3D reference information; online stage: relocalise via frustum overlap + matching; rotation error and f error figures exist (SIFT variant ~0.12 deg median/mean rotation error in one table, dataset not verified) — [PTZ-Calib](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09075)
- BroadTrack's evidence on tripod vs per-frame positions: with the tripod constraint the focal point stays within the neighbourhood of T, whereas per-frame NBJW centres wander up to 20 m within a 30 s clip; single-frame metrics barely move (JaC5 54.1 -> 54.9 with T alone) — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721)
- Puwein et al. refine everything (intrinsics, extrinsics, trajectories, ground lines) by final bundle adjustment — [Puwein WACV 2012 listing](https://mlanthology.org/wacv/2012/puwein2012wacv-ptz)
- WorldPose: camera location constant per clip, per-frame f, principal point, k1-k3 and rotation optimised jointly with player keypoint reprojection and smoothness terms (Adam, lr 1e-3, loss weights 1 field / 0.5 player), regulariser weights tuned manually per clip — [WorldPose](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02771v2); supplement notes low field-marking reprojection error does not imply low player reprojection error.

### Inferences
- A match-level BA that is plausible to build from these parts: shared parameters {T (or C), δ, k1 as function of f (Sinha-style), principal point} + per-frame {pan, tilt, roll≈0, f}; observations = line/keypoint reprojection (anchor frames) + flow-derived ray correspondences (all frames). Expect the biggest practical gain from the shared distortion/centre, not from per-frame refinement.
- Ambiguity warnings: f vs depth/position (vertigo effect, BroadTrack) and radial distortion vs zoom-dependent f (Sinha) mean the shared parameters need a prior or must be fitted on frames with lots of visible lines.

### Gaps
- No numbers on accuracy gains of match-wide BA vs per-frame estimation on football broadcast video were found. The user would need to measure this on their own labelled frames.
- Puwein et al. accuracy and Chen/Little base-estimation accuracy (error in C) were not retrieved.

## 4. Temporal smoothing and temporal losses in learned methods

### Takeaway
Smoothing in practice is simple: Savitzky-Golay (GSR 2024 winner), EKF/particle filters (PTZ-SLAM, Citraro), MRF/dynamic programming over candidate homographies plus L1 trajectory stabilisation (Sharma), or smoothness terms inside the optimiser (WorldPose, BroadTrack). Gains are modest in per-frame metrics (+2 IoU points for MRF) but important for downstream stability. I found no learned method with an explicit temporal loss for calibration.

### Cited Findings
- Constructor Tech (SoccerNet GSR 2024 winner, GS-HOTA 63.81): per-frame SegFormer-based camera-parameter regressor + keypoint-based brute-force refinement, then Savitzky-Golay smoothing limited to a maximum adjustment of ±2 deg and ±2 m with predictions delayed by 15 frames (0.5 s) so the filter sees past and future — [Golovkin et al., arXiv 2504.06357 (full text read)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.06357)
- Sharma et al.: per-frame k=5 candidate homographies from dictionary NN search; MRF via dynamic programming with data term + smoothness term ||H_s_t - H_s_{t-1}||^2 over normalised 8 parameters; second stage L1-norm camera stabilisation (piecewise constant / linear / parabolic segments, motivated by professional cameraman behaviour). IoU 85% -> 87% with MRF — [arXiv 1703.01437](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.01437)
- Lu et al.: EKF over (pan, tilt, f + velocities) with velocity motion model; physically meaningful parameters make the uncertainties easy to set (vs. homography EKF "hard to tune") — [arXiv 1907.08816](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.08816)
- Citraro: particle filter (300 particles) is critical: 1.68 -> 6.96 deg error when removed (basketball) — [ar5iv 2003.14109](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.14109)
- BroadTrack: optical flow and the tripod prior make pan/focal-length trajectories smoother than NBJW's per-frame output (qualitative figures); no separate filter — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721)
- WorldPose: base objective gave jittery distortion curves, so a frame-to-frame smoothness term on f and distortion was added — [WorldPose supplement](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02771v2)
- TS-WorldCup (Chu et al., CVPRW 2022): 3812 annotated field images from time-sequence frames of 43 videos of World Cup 2014/2018, built to allow temporal evaluation; KpSFR itself is a per-image method (search evidence did not show temporal modelling) — [KpSFR project page](https://ericsujw.github.io/KpSFR); [CVPRW paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022W/CVSports/papers/Chu_Sports_Field_Registration_via_Keypoints-Aware_Label_Condition_CVPRW_2022_paper.pdf)
- PnLCalib / "No Bells, Just Whistles" (arXiv 2404.08401, journal version CVIU vol. 267, April 2026): optimisation-based, per-frame; states recent approaches leverage broadcast sequentiality to enforce temporal consistency between frames' homographies — [arXiv 2404.08401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08401)
- A July 2026 SoccerNet-GSR pipeline uses "PnLCalib-based camera calibration with a temporal bridge" and "shot-change gating", but the paper gives no detail and no calibration ablation — [arXiv 2607.11548 (abstract-level, page read)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11548v1)
- Over-smoothing risk: a learned sliding-window temporal refiner (27 frames) lowered local MPJPE 22% but hurt the competition score, so the authors kept only Gaussian smoothing (this is about pose refinement, not calibration, but is a caution on learned temporal refiners) — [SMART](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.31551)

### Inferences
- A Savitzky-Golay / RTS-smoother pass on (pan, tilt, f, k1) with an offline 0.5 s lookahead is cheap and has competition-winning precedent; bounded corrections (±2 deg) avoid smoothing over genuine camera jumps or cuts (smooth only within shot segments).
- Smoothing should be done in physical parameters, not homography entries, as in PTZ-SLAM/BroadTrack.

### Gaps
- No "one-euro filter" use on camera parameters in football calibration papers was found (it is a generic technique; no source).
- No paper with an explicit temporal-consistency *loss* in a learned calibration net (TS-WorldCup/KpSFR, Nie et al. WACV 2021 abstracts do not show one). Nie et al.'s paper was not read in full.

## 5. Self-supervised / test-time adaptation using temporal or photometric consistency

### Takeaway
I found no published sports-calibration method that does test-time adaptation or self-supervised training on unlabeled match video using photometric warping across frames. Building blocks exist: unsupervised photometric homography networks, self-supervised shape alignment for field registration, photometric/flow-consistency terms inside per-clip optimisation.

### Cited Findings
- Nguyen et al. (RA-L 2018): unsupervised deep homography with average L1 photometric loss between warped and target images, no labels; beats a supervised twin on UAV data (not video sequences, not sports) — [arXiv 1709.03966](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.03966)
- Shi et al. (WACV 2022): self-supervised shape alignment for sports field registration; mines training data from natural images and edge maps, iterative estimator guided by a score-regression network, fine-tunes with little labelled data — [paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2022/papers/Shi_Self-Supervised_Shape_Alignment_for_Sports_Field_Registration_WACV_2022_paper.pdf)
- WorldPose: uses sparse-LK flow consistency on field markings and a smoothness prior as unsupervised regularisers *inside a per-clip optimisation* (not a network); distance-field photometric refinement is described only for static cameras — [WorldPose](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02771v2)
- TVCalib's calibration module needs no training data (iteratively minimises segment reprojection error), so it can serve as a pseudo-label generator — [TVCalib](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.11709); Constructor Tech used a modified TVCalib to build a labelled camera-parameter dataset for real footage — [arXiv 2504.06357](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.06357)

### Inferences
- A feasible self-training loop for this project (unvalidated): run the tracker/BA pipeline above over whole matches to produce dense, temporally consistent pseudo-labels (accept frames with high line-IoU), then fine-tune the per-frame network on those; photometric/flow consistency between neighbouring frames can gate or weight pseudo-labels. This is a design suggestion, not a reported result.

### Gaps
- No test-time adaptation paper for sports field registration found (searched twice; only snippets). A Google patent (US12211222B2, "video-based sports field registration") mentions pseudo-labelling; claims not checked.

## 6. Shot boundary detection, camera-type classification, deciding which frames are calibratable

### Takeaway
Pipelines separate "is this the main wide camera?" from "is the calibration confident?". SoccerNet-v2 provides 13-way camera-type labels and shot boundaries (a transition every ~9 s on average); in practice cheap histogram/TransNetV2 cut detection plus a main-camera classifier gate the frames, and a line-overlap confidence score decides per-frame whether to keep tracking or re-initialise. Within a shot, calibration is propagated; across a cut it must be re-anchored.

### Cited Findings
- SoccerNet-v2: 13 camera shot types annotated for 200 games (main camera center / left / right, main behind the goal, goal-line-technology camera, spider cam, close-ups (player/referee, side staff, corner, behind goal), inside the goal, public, other), ~1 transition every 9 s, replay flag and transition type (abrupt / fading / logo) per annotation; benchmarks for 13-class camera segmentation and boundary detection; PySceneDetect "Content", scikit-video Histogram/Intensity and CALF baselines — Histogram leads overall boundary detection (abrupt strong, logo transitions strong), CALF best on fading — [SoccerNet-v2 (full text read)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.13367); label definitions in the [supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021W/CVSports/supplemental/Deliege_SoccerNet-v2_A_Dataset_CVPRW_2021_supplemental.pdf)
- "Main camera center" = "camera shown most of the time"; left/right "16 m" cameras are also high wide cameras at different fixed positions (distinct camera centres, so a per-camera T is needed) — [SoccerNet-v2 supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021W/CVSports/supplemental/Deliege_SoccerNet-v2_A_Dataset_CVPRW_2021_supplemental.pdf); BroadTrack uses separate default focal points for main, 16m left/right and high-behind-goal — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721)
- TransNet V2: shot-transition detector, F1 77.9 (ClipShots), 96.2 (BBC Planet Earth), 93.9 (RAI); original paper claims far above real-time on a modest GPU (exact fps not verified; secondary summaries conflict) — [arXiv 2008.04838](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.04838)
- Sharma et al.: classify each frame into 5 classes (top zoom-out, top zoom-in, ground zoom-out, ground zoom-in, miscellaneous/crowd) with BoW-SIFT + linear SVM over a 40-frame temporal window and temporal smoothing: 98% accuracy on top zoom-out when trained on 45 min and tested on 45 min from another match; ~400 shot changes in a 45-min video — [arXiv 1703.01437](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.01437)
- BroadTrack uses calibration confidence rather than a shot detector: s = Jaccard(markings mask, projected template); on sn-calibration (all camera types) the 2-point re-init is only evaluated on views with s < 0.2 (a score filter, completeness 69.8%): JaC5 75.25 / JaC10 86.8 at 960x540 vs NBJW 73.7 / 86.7 at 77.5% completeness — i.e. the confidence score acts as the "calibratable" gate — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721) (note: the paper's text says "score s < 0.2" for views kept; as printed, direction of the inequality appears to refer to its own error-style use of the score and should be checked in the paper before reuse)
- Lu et al. have no explicit cut handling; relocalisation via the pan-tilt forest handles tracking loss — [arXiv 1907.08816](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.08816)
- A 2026 SoccerNet-GSR pipeline lists "shot-change gating" as a component (no detail) — [arXiv 2607.11548](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11548v1)

### Inferences
- Gate = (camera-type classifier says main wide) AND (calibration line-IoU above threshold). Within a segment between detected cuts, propagate; at a cut back to a previously seen main-camera shot, re-anchor from the detector and reuse the per-camera tripod prior. Replays of the main camera are the same physical camera, so same T applies; reverse-angle and 16 m cameras need their own T.
- Because shots are ~9 s on average, and the main camera is the majority class, most frames are in segments of several seconds long; this is exactly the regime where propagation from few anchors pays off.

### Gaps
- No paper found that evaluates "calibratable frame" classification directly on SoccerNet; the TransNetV2 speed and SoccerNet camera-type classifier accuracies could not be verified (Table 3 of SoccerNet-v2 was garbled in extraction; exact mIoU/mAP values not recorded).

## 7. Practical libraries: ECC, flow, matchers — what they cost on CPU vs GPU

### Takeaway
Evidence for football specifically: sparse Lucas-Kanade + RANSAC (CPU-cheap) and RAFT-small + outlier filtering (GPU) are equally accurate for rotation propagation, while unmasked OpenCV ECC homography is clearly worse. Learned matchers (LightGlue, LoFTR) have GPU timings in the 20-200 ms/pair range; I found no football-specific benchmark for them and almost no desktop-CPU numbers.

### Cited Findings
- ECC (OpenCV `findTransformECC`, MOTION_HOMOGRAPHY) on soccer: worst of four trackers, 0.107 deg vs 0.041-0.043 for LK/RAFT, because players dominate and ECC cannot ignore them — [SMART](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.31551). (OpenCV's `findTransformECC` has an `inputMask` argument that could in principle mask players; not tested in any source found and docs page not retrievable.)
- RAFT-small + MAD: 55 ms/frame, RAFT-large 110 ms/frame, no accuracy gain (hardware unspecified) — [SMART](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.31551). Original RAFT paper: ~9 fps for the full model and ~20 fps for the small variant at 1088x436 on a GTX 1080 Ti (as reported by a secondary article, not the paper's table) — [forasoft summary](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/ai-for-video-engineering/articles-ai/optical-flow-raft-vs-lucas-kanade); paper https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.12039
- Pyramidal Lucas-Kanade is what BroadTrack uses for its flow term, alongside GPU nets for field detection; the full system is 16 fps at 1080p on 2x RTX 4090, unoptimised — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721)
- LightGlue: 44.2 ms/pair (31.4 ms adaptive) vs SuperGlue 70 ms and LoFTR 181 ms on MegaDepth-1500 as quoted in a benchmark snippet (hardware not stated) — [LightGlue paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13643) / [snippet source list](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.04765); Efficient LoFTR tables (RTX 3090 + 14-core Xeon, 480 px short side): LoFTR 83.2 ms (Aachen), SuperPoint+SuperGlue 55.9 ms — [Efficient LoFTR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.04765)
- The only LightGlue CPU number found: ~666 ms/pair on an RK3588 embedded ARM CPU (FastGlue variant, ~17 ms on GPU) — [VMatcher-era search hit / FastGlue, ISPRS 2026](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/XI-2-2026/45/2026/isprs-annals-XI-2-2026-45-2026.pdf) (snippet only; not a desktop CPU)
- Older classical pipelines for reference: two-point + SIFT 0.2 s/frame SIFT on a 2018 CPU — [two-point paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.09005); PTZ-Calib online stage 0.68 s/frame (0.34 s matching) — [PTZ-Calib](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09075)
- Code available: BroadTrack (C++/Docker/GPU) https://github.com/evs-broadcast/BroadTrack; two-point https://github.com/lood339/two_point_calib; PTZ-SLAM https://github.com/lulufa390/Pan-tilt-zoom-SLAM (reported to crash after ~200 frames by BroadTrack authors); PTZ-Calib https://github.com/gjgjh/PTZ-Calib; TVCalib https://github.com/MM4SPA/tvcalib; PnLCalib / NBJW per-frame calibrators (arXiv 2404.08401)

### Inferences
- On a GPU-less laptop, the sensible baseline is sparse LK (cv2.calcOpticalFlowPyrLK) + RANSAC rotation/H fit with player masking, running on a downscaled grayscale frame; RAFT-small/LightGlue belong on the desktop RTX 2070 via the Prefect gpu pool (project memory).
- Use ECC only with a background mask (if at all), and as a sub-pixel refiner after LK; do not use it as the primary tracker.

### Gaps
- No verified desktop-CPU timings for OpenCV ECC, DIS/Farneback flow, LoFTR or LightGlue; RAFT timings are secondary and hardware-ambiguous. These would need a quick local benchmark on the footy-track clips.
- TransNetV2 inference speed not verified from the paper.
