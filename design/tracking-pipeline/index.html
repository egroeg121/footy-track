    Tracking pipeline: what we have, and where it should go¶

    Status: DRAFT for review · Date: 2026-11-08 · Code reviewed: main @ 26d2506

    Two pictures. Part 1 is the detection and tracking path as it really is in code today, from a broadcast clip to whatever comes out the other end. Part 2 is the proposed end-to-end system: plays tracked on a top-down pitch in metres, with team and player identity, instead of boxes in broadcast pixels. Part 3 is a phased plan with the labels, effort and success metric for each phase.

    Every number is marked measured (with its denominator) or assumed. The project has already been burnt by a ball mAP quoted off 44 instances (pipeline overview), so treat unmarked confidence with suspicion.

    This page builds on, and does not replace, the stage docs: pipeline overview, player re-id, tracking, projection, output and system_design.md. Where it disagrees with them it says so in Relationship to existing design docs.

    What comes out today: per-clip JSONL of boxes (machine_labels/), per-clip tracklets (tracklets/), and human verdict logs. There is no ball trajectory, no team_id, no player_id, and no pitch coordinate anywhere. The footy-track console script prints "Hello from footy-track!".

    The gaps, in one list¶

    1. No ball tracker. The ball is a set of independent per-frame boxes. The only ball tracker in a running path is VitTrack in the labeller, which holds the ball on 5% of frames (9/176, measured, KB footy-labeller-gotchas).
    2. The producers of the data we rely on are not in git: the RT-DETR training command, the batch inference that made the 2.77M machine labels, and the filter from machine labels to ball_candidates/.
    3. Identity output is unused. link_proposals/ and identity_labels/ are written and read by nothing.
    4. The team gate is inert. linker.candidate_links skips cross-team pairs only when both tracklets have a team (identity/linker.py:125), and no code sets it.
    5. No calibration and no projection. The store has a homography column (feature_store/schema.py:158) that nothing writes, and PitchProjector is called only from tests.
    6. The ball benchmark is narrow. v11-val has 211 balls, but 156 of them (74%) come from one 10-second clip (arsenal_example). All machine labels are one match.

    Part 2: proposed end-to-end tracking of plays¶

    The target: a broadcast match in, and out of it, for every frame where the pitch is visible, every player and the ball on a 105 x 68 m pitch with a team, a player identity where we can get one, and a confidence. Possessions, passes and plays are built on top of that world state, not on broadcast pixels.

    Calibration per frame, as a state machine¶

    Calibration is where broadcast footage fights back: wide shots show plenty of lines, close-ups show grass and a player, and replays show a different camera. The homography stage must say which case it is in rather than emit a confident wrong matrix.

    stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> NoPitch
  NoPitch: No pitch view. Close-up, replay, crowd. No H, positions omitted
  Fitted: H fitted from keypoints and smoothed each frame. Enough matches, low reprojection error
  Carried: H carried from last fit by camera motion. Zoomed in, few lines
  Lost: Carried too long or drifted. Positions NaN, flagged
  NoPitch --> Fitted: wide view, enough keypoints
  Fitted --> Carried: too few keypoints
  Carried --> Fitted: keypoints return
  Carried --> Lost: drift check fails or N frames
  Lost --> Fitted: keypoints return
  Fitted --> NoPitch: cut or view change
  Carried --> NoPitch: cut or view change

    Part 3: phased plan¶

    Effort is for one person: S is days, M is one to two weeks, L is several weeks. These are estimates, not measurements.

    Phase Goal Builds Labels and data it needs Effort Success metric
    0 A simple inference pipeline in git (separate PR) Video to broadcast clips to RT-DETR at 1280 to per-clip detections and LapTracker tracklets, as one command None new; reproduce the existing machine_labels/ on one clip S Matches the existing machine labels on a sample clip; runs end to end from a clean checkout
    1 Trustworthy image-space tracking A committed ball eval; the ball tracker over machine labels; a player tracking eval (player_eval/, mirroring ball_eval/); team clustering wired in as a constraint Dense held-out ball clips with no_ball markers, at least 2 matches (~1.5-3k balls, assumed); the untouched v11 test split (243 balls); purity labels on 10 clips; detector run over a second match M Ball recall and precision at a fixed operating point on the held-out multi-match set, reported per match with instance counts; tracklet purity ≥ 0.95 and ID switches ≤ 1 per 1,000 player-frames (player_reid.md targets); team accuracy ≥ 0.98
    2 Players and ball on a 2D pitch Pitch keypoint detector, per-frame homography with the state machine above, PitchProjector wired up, world-state store, a top-down replay Pitch keypoint labels on a few hundred frames across views and at least 2 stadiums (assumed); ~50 held-out frames with hand-clicked correspondences L Reprojection error in pixels on held-out keypoints; position error in metres on known landmarks (penalty spots, box corners); share of broadcast frames with a usable H; player speeds stay physically plausible
    3 Who is who across shots ReID-trained embedding in score_links.py; team + number + appearance identity resolution; jersey numbers from humans, OCR only if measured legibility supports it Jersey numbers per tracklet (human, seconds each); merge verdicts; a second match for any generalisation claim L Identity accuracy: share of player-frames with the correct player_id on labelled clips; re-id mAP within a match with the gallery size stated
    4 Plays and the ball in 3D Possession, passes and plays from world state; ball height from ballistic fits Hand-labelled events (pass start and end, shots) on a few matches; no height ground truth exists L Pass detection precision and recall against labelled events; possession accuracy per second; for height, consistency checks (landing points against later ground contacts)

    Order. Phase 1 first: it reuses labels and machine output we already have, and every later phase needs its evals. Phase 2 can start in parallel once Phase 0 lands, because calibration does not depend on tracking. Phase 3 waits on the player_reid.md R7 question: is per-player identity needed at all, or are team and pitch position enough? Phase 4 comes last.

    Top risks¶

    1. Broadcast cuts break tracking. Every cut ends every track, and replays show the same players from another angle. Within a clip the splitter gives cut-free shots (measured on 5 clips), so cuts turn into a cross-shot identity problem (Phase 3), not a tracker problem. A temporal ball detector must fall back to a single frame at cuts.
    2. Homography fails on zoomed shots. Close-ups show too few landmarks for a fit. The plan is to carry H through pan and zoom by camera motion and to mark positions NaN once drift is too high, never to emit a confident wrong position (consistent with projection.md §5).
    3. The ball is ~11 px at 1280. Recall is 0.59 on a narrow benchmark, and the misses cluster in long gaps. A tracker can raise precision a lot and recall a little. Recall needs more diverse labels (other matches, other camera angles).
    4. An airborne ball projects to the wrong place. A homography maps the ground plane. A ball 3 m up, seen from a camera about 25 m high and 60 m away, lands roughly 5-10 m from its true ground point (geometry estimate). Until Phase 4, the ball's pitch position is only trustworthy when it is on or near the ground, and must carry a flag saying so.
    5. One match. All machine labels, the team measurement and the identity gallery come from Arsenal v Man City. No claim generalises until a second match is processed.

    Open questions¶

    1. Which phase first after Phase 0: the ball tracker and evals (Phase 1), or calibration and the top-down view (Phase 2)?
    2. How ambitious on the ball in 3D: a 2D ground position with an "airborne" flag, or real height estimation?
    3. Jersey numbers: human anchors only (as player_reid.md §3.7 recommends), or OCR on the best frame of large tracklets? pipeline_overview.md says about 40% of tracklets have a frame at least 110 px tall and a human has read numbers at 102 px; that is the case for measuring OCR legibility before ruling it out.
    4. Is persistent per-player identity needed downstream, or are team, role and pitch position enough (player_reid.md R7, pipeline_overview.md Q5)?
    5. Calibration model: train our own keypoint detector on our labels, or start from a public pretrained pitch-calibration model and fine-tune (licence and fit to our footage to check)?
    6. Where should the world state live: the DuckDB feature store, or the per-stage parquet files from output.md and projection.md?
