# Norwich (held-out) unsupervised ball diagnostics and dense-label queue

Branch `exp/norwich-label-queue` (from `exp/offline-ball-tracking` fc05732). Generated by `scripts/norwich_video_cuts.py`, `scripts/norwich_diagnostics.py`, `scripts/norwich_camera_check.py`; machine-readable results in `norwich_unsupervised_diagnostics.json`, the queue in `norwich_label_queue.csv`.

**Held-out match. `arsenal_norwich` is really Arsenal v Nottingham Forest (scoreboard `ARS`/`NFO`, TNT Sports broadcast). Never use it for training or tuning.** The 76 clips cover broadcast frames only: 25,694 frames (17.1 min) spread over source minutes 5.4 to 40.4 of the 40-minute proxy video (`segments.json`), not 40 continuous minutes. Detections: RT-DETR-L v11 @1280, conf >= 0.1, 674,087 rows (`machine_labels_norwich/`).

There is **no ground truth** here. Every number below is a self-consistency diagnostic of the detector and solver, not an accuracy. "Mean p" is the calibrated Ball Check precision of the *accepted* candidates (`solver.calibrated_p`), whose knots were measured on Man City, so it is a prior, not a Norwich measurement.

## Setup

- B0: top-1 ball candidate per frame, kept if conf >= 0.4 (the dev-selected tau of the Man City baseline).
- B1: the `camera_on` solver config from `docs/experiments/offline_ball_baseline.md` section 6 (min_conf 0.3, bias 1, kappa 1.5, lam_pos 0.5, vmax 40, r0 12, static_px 3, max_gap 25, camera compensation on, Kalman fill of gaps <= 10 frames between nodes with calibrated p >= 0.8). Parameters are in the json (`b1_params`), unchanged.
- Cuts: the solver's player-box cut flag, OR-ed with an independent video-based flag (gray-histogram L1 > 0.1 and 64x36 thumbnail MAD > 2.5; frames 1-4 of each clip ignored, see below). B1 links may not cross either.
- Diagnostics: *present* = B1 emits a point (detections plus interpolated fills); *mean p* = mean calibrated p over accepted detection nodes; *segments* = maximal runs of consecutive present frames; *gap* = an absent run bounded by present frames on both sides (leading/trailing absence not counted); *disagree* = B0 has a confident (>= 0.4) top-1 and B1 either moves it more than 10 px ("moved") or has no point ("dropped"); *multi* = frame has >= 2 candidates >= 0.3 more than 30 px apart.
- Windows: 250-frame (10 s) tiles per clip (last partial tile kept if >= 50 frames); 129 tiles, 68 of them full, in the json (`tiles_250`).
- Decode check: OpenCV 5.0 decoded all 76 clips correctly (no black frames, mean gray >= 69, frame counts equal `segments.json` and the label manifest), so no PyAV fallback was needed.

Reading: the detector is confident on most frames of this broadcast: a candidate >= 0.7 exists on 66% of all frames and B1 is present on 82.6% (the Man City 0.85 coverage was measured on hand-picked GT frames, so the two are not comparable). The accepted-node confidence is tightly clustered (median 0.79, p90 0.84, global max 0.87): RT-DETR confidences on this footage saturate around 0.8, so the calibrated-p knots at 0.85+ are never reached and "p >= 0.9" mostly just means "conf >= 0.7". B0 and B1 mostly agree: they differ on only 4.0% of frames, and 71% of those are B1 *dropping* a confident top-1 (typically an isolated top-1 with no motion-consistent neighbours), not choosing a different candidate. Precision of those drops is unknown without labels; the contested stratum below is where to find out.

## Things that look off

- **Camera-motion estimation is mediocre on this broadcast.** On 6 clips (250 frames each) the player-box translation was compared with phase correlation of the video itself (`norwich_camera_check.py`): x-correlation 0.94 (seg000), 0.91 (seg060), 0.83 (seg026), 0.74 (seg024), 0.59 (seg044) and **0.25 (seg010)**; y-correlation 0.1-0.6 (y motion is small). Median |dx| from players underestimates the video (5.5 vs 7.1 px on seg060). Likely cause: on the high TNT angle the crowd is detected as `player` (median 29-45 "players" per frame on the short seg062-seg073 clips versus 15-20 on typical clips; seg065, seg066 and seg068 were viewed and show a packed crowd behind the goal), so crowd boxes vote for zero motion. Consequence for B1: compensation errors of 3-6 px/frame are small against vmax 40 but would hurt link scoring on fast pans; the camera=off variant was within noise on Man City, so this is a caveat rather than a failure. The player-box cut detector never fired (0 cuts, estimate valid on 100% of frames), which is consistent with clips being pre-split but means it is untested here.
- **No genuine shot cuts inside clips.** The video detector's strongest responses are at frames 1-4 of several clips (dissolve-in transient, e.g. seg000 f1 histogram distance 0.56 with a `REFCAM` overlay visible), which are ignored. Beyond those it flags 10 weak events: seg002 (1), seg031 (4, f40-47, gradual), seg044 (4, f1840-1842) and seg074 (f168); none looked like a hard cut in the stats, and they were not eyeballed. Windows start at frame >= 10 to avoid the dissolve-in. Only 3 of 129 tiles contain a flagged event.
- **Half the clips are too short for a 10 s window.** 38 of 76 clips are < 250 frames (5,373 frames in total), so the queue can only draw from the 38 longer clips (about 20,300 frames).
- **Non-play-in-the-ball sense:** seg073 (64 frames, 33:00 on the match clock, Arsenal 1-0) is a goal celebration with a player down: B1 present 9%, max conf 0.61. seg065/066/068 (62-82 frames, ~28-29 min) are real match play (attacking corner / set piece near the goal line) where B1 is present on 34-41%; the ball is tiny and sits against the touchline/crowd. These are not non-match clips, just hard ones. No replays or studio/graphic-only clips were seen in the sample inspected (a handful of clips and 2 contact sheets were viewed), but not every clip was.
- Detector provenance: the label manifest says `heldout: true, do_not_train: true`.

## Label queue (24 windows, 250 frames each, no overlap, at most 2 per clip, no cut inside)

Each window is [start_frame, end_frame] inclusive within one clip. Strata were assigned from fixed rules on the window's diagnostics (applied to sliding windows with stride 25 from frame 10; all required median player count >= 5-8 as a wide-shot proxy): *easy* B1 present >= 0.9 and mean p >= 0.9; *gappy* >= 1 gap of >= 10 frames, B1 present >= 0.4; *contested* disagree >= 6% or multi >= 25%, present >= 0.3; *low-evidence* <= 35% of frames with a candidate >= 0.7 while >= 50% have one >= 0.1. 383 windows qualified (easy 222, gappy 144, contested 77, low-evidence 46; windows can qualify for several). Because Norwich confidences are high, the low-evidence and contested thresholds were set relative to the Norwich tile distribution (the first draft used Man City-style absolute values and found no qualifying window). For each stratum the match timeline (source minutes 5.4-40.4) is cut into 6 equal bins and the highest-scoring window is taken from each bin, with empty bins filled by the farthest-in-time candidate. Low-evidence windows still cluster at minutes 16-17 (seg023-seg025), where the pool is concentrated.

## Caveats

- No ground truth: nothing here says B1 is *right*. High present/mean-p windows may still contain a confidently detected wrong object (the Man City GT-frame table shows the 0.4-0.6 conf band is only about a third precise (10/29), and a static distractor such as an advertising-board ball or a ball in a player's hand reads as a track). The easy stratum is where labelling will quantify that.
- The calibrated-p knots were measured on Man City; the Norwich confidence distribution is shifted up (median node conf 0.79), so mean p near 0.95 should be read as an upper-bound prior.
- The windows are chosen from the solver's own diagnostics, so labelling them gives a stratified, solver-informed sample, not an unbiased estimate of overall accuracy. To estimate overall B1 recall/precision, label a few random windows as well (not included here).
- All 24 sheets were generated with OpenCV on CPU; at most one heavy process ran at a time with OMP_NUM_THREADS=2.
