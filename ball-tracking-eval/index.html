    Ball tracking evaluation¶

    How well does the ball tracker follow the real ball? This page describes the measurement (footy_track.ball_eval.detection_replay and scripts/ball_tracking_eval.py), the ground truth it uses, and the first results for the three detection-based trackers. It is the first scored ball measurement of the tracker; it does not replace the detector evaluation in T-10 (see TODO), which scores the detector itself on the v11 test split.

    What is measured¶

    The tracker never sees pixels, only the ball candidates the detector produced (confidence 0.1 or more, nothing filtered). So the measurement splits in two:

    1. cache runs the detector once per clip, on a GPU, and writes every ball candidate per frame to <clip>.jsonl (one header line with the clip, frame size, weights, image size and confidence floor, then {"f": frame, "c": [[x, y, w, h, conf], ...]} per frame). Same weights and settings as the inference pipeline (the rtdetr_1920_v11 checkpoint at image size 1280, confidence 0.1).
    2. score replays any tracker over the cache on a CPU (about 7 seconds for 26 clips and three trackers) and compares its output with the human ball marks. Change a tracker, rerun score; the detector is never rerun.

    review is a third, proxy-only command for runs without ground truth: it counts how many frames each tracker changes relative to the first one and how many of its ball positions are in the stands (normalised y under 0.12).

    python -m footy_track.scripts.ball_tracking_eval cache  --videos <clips> --gt <gt> --out <cache> --weights <best.pt>
python -m footy_track.scripts.ball_tracking_eval score  --cache <cache> --gt <gt> --split split.json \
    --trackers online,bidirectional,viterbi --radii 10,25 [--per-clip] [--param viterbi.enter_cost=6]
python -m footy_track.scripts.ball_tracking_eval review --runs <dir of run folders>

    split.json maps each canonical clip id to "seen" or "held_out".

    Ground truth rules¶

    Marks come from the labeller sidecars (ball_gt_marks/<clip>.jsonl).

    • A ball mark is a row whose first tag is in_play_ball or out_of_play_ball.
    • Only marks tagged labeller (placed or confirmed by a person in the labeller) count as ground truth. This is the headline tier, human.
    • Marks a machine propagated and a person later checked (vittrack plus human_checked) are scored in a separate human+propagated tier. They are not independent of the tracking family, so do not headline them.
    • yolo and sam3 marks, and rows without a provenance tag, are never ground truth.
    • A no_ball row means the ball is not visible in that frame. not_broadcast frames and frames with no row at all are not scored.
    • Sidecars are merged by canonical clip id (footy_track.clip_id), because one clip has several names (for example mancity_seg010 and arsenal_mancity_seg010). If a frame has several marks, the nearest counts.

    The ground truth is small: 646 human ball frames and 37 human no-ball frames over 26 clips. Most clips have between 1 and 60 marks; the huge sidecars (thousands of rows) are almost entirely player boxes and propagated ball boxes, so only a few dozen of their rows are human ball marks. Marks are sparse (isolated frames), so a clip scores only on its marked frames; the tracker still runs over the whole clip. The marks were mostly made while reviewing detector output, so frames where the detector found nothing at all are probably under-represented. Treat the recall figures as optimistic.

    Metrics¶

    On every frame with a ball mark, at pixel radius R (native resolution; R = 10 and 25):

    • HIT: the prediction centre is within R of a mark.
    • WRONG: there is a prediction but it is farther than R. This is the "following the wrong object" failure and the number to watch.
    • MISS: no prediction.

    On every no_ball frame, FALSE means a prediction exists.

    • precision = HIT / (HIT + WRONG + FALSE)
    • recall = HIT / ball-marked frames
    • wrong / pred = WRONG / (HIT + WRONG): how often a made prediction is the wrong object
    • HIT and WRONG are split into detected (d) and interpolated (i) predictions.
    • detector any-cand is the detector upper bound: the ball-marked frames where any candidate of confidence 0.1 or more is within R. The gap between this and recall is tracker loss (and interpolation can push recall above it).

    Leakage: seen and held-out clips¶

    A clip is seen if any frame of it, or of its source footage, is in the detector training data (Roboflow dataset v11, any split), held-out otherwise. Checked from the dataset image names, plus a check that the whole-match frames in v11 (named by match frame number, sampled every 5th frame) fall in the clip's time span; arsenal_mancity_part093 is seen for that reason.

    • Held-out (6 clips, 119 human ball frames, 7 no-ball frames): arsenal_mancity_seg031 (53), arsenal_bournemouth_1st_seg035 (40), arsenal_bournemouth_1st_seg031 (11), _seg015 (9), _seg021 (5), _seg060 (1).
    • Seen (20 clips, 527 human ball frames, 30 no-ball frames): the rest, among them arsenal_example, which has 157 images in the v11 validation split, and the two large single-clip sources of v11, astonvilla_seg080 and bournemouth_1st_seg070.

    Seen numbers measure how well the tracker uses detections the model has effectively memorised, so they overstate real performance. The tracker parameters below were tuned on the seen clips only. The held-out set is small (119 ball frames, 78% of them from two clips), so differences of a few frames are noise.

    What the numbers say:

    • Most of the held-out loss is the detector, not the tracker: only 74 of 119 ball frames have any candidate within 25 px (62%). No tracker can fix that.
    • Held-out WRONG falls from 30 to 20 (bidirectional) or 18 (viterbi) and precision rises from 0.68 to 0.74-0.75. Bidirectional keeps online's recall within 3 frames; viterbi gives up recall (0.50) for precision.
    • On the seen clips both new trackers cut WRONG by half or more (44 to 21 and 7).
    • The held-out gain comes mostly from two clips (arsenal_mancity_seg031 and arsenal_bournemouth_1st_seg031) and is within what 119 frames can distinguish; treat it as encouraging, not proven. The default stays online.
    • arsenal_bournemouth_1st_seg035 is bad for every tracker (about 17 of 40 frames wrong): its ball is often high in the air near the stands, where no tracker trusts it and where the detector often finds nothing. This is also why a "no ball in the stands" penalty is not safe: real balls go there.

    Per-clip and pooled results (including the human+propagated tier, where bidirectional reaches precision 0.97 and recall 0.93 on seen clips) come from score --per-clip.

    The two new trackers¶

    Both are offline (they need the whole clip's candidates), write the same ball_track.jsonl rows, and are selected with --ball-tracker (InferenceConfig.ball_tracker, default online). The --ball-* threshold flags configure only the online tracker; the offline ones use the defaults below. The web app is unchanged and keeps online.

    Bidirectional (ball_trackers/bidirectional.py)¶

    Runs DetectionBallTracker forward and on the time-reversed candidates, then merges per frame:

    • neither pass has a ball: no ball;
    • only one pass has a detected ball: take it; a bridge (interpolated frames) that only one pass makes is dropped (fill="agree"; fill="any" keeps it);
    • both have a ball within 60 px: agree; keep the detected one (forward if both are);
    • they disagree: keep the pass whose track (the run of frames with one track id in that pass) has more support. Support is the sum of its detected confidences (support="conf_sum"; "strong_count", the number of detections at or above the start confidence, scored identically here).

    Parameters: online defaults except max_missed 6 and growth_px 15 (agree_px 60, fill agree, support conf_sum).

    Viterbi (ball_trackers/viterbi.py)¶

    Picks the single best path through the whole clip. Each frame offers its top 8 candidates (confidence 0.1 or more) or no ball. A path scores:

    • per chosen candidate: the log-odds that a candidate of that confidence is the ball, interpolated from the Ball Check precision bands (0.04 at 0.1-0.2, 0.42 at 0.5-0.6, 0.79 at 0.6-0.7, 0.96 at 0.7-0.8, near 1 above), plus a bias of 1.0;
    • minus a move cost between consecutive chosen candidates g frames and d px apart: 3 * (d / (30 + 40 g))^2, capped at 8 (so a camera cut is allowed, never free), plus 0.3 per skipped frame;
    • minus 4 to start a track and 0.5 to end one.

    Candidates more than 12 frames apart are never linked, and gaps inside a track are filled by linear interpolation only up to that limit. There is an optional top_penalty for candidates in the stands (normalised y under top_y, 0.12); it is off because the grid search gave no consistent gain and the seg035 clip shows real balls up there. No pitch mask is used. It runs in a fraction of a second per clip.

    Tuning¶

    Light grid searches on the seen clips only, objective HIT minus twice (WRONG + FALSE) at 25 px: viterbi emission_bias 0.5-3.5, enter_cost 2-6, jump_cost 4-8, gap_cost 0.1-0.3, move_weight 1-3, max_gap 8-20, speed_px 25-70, base_px 20-50 (the surface is flat; the chosen values are inside a plateau, not at its best corner); bidirectional fill, agree_px, support, start_conf, jump_conf, max_missed 3-25, base_radius_px, growth_px. Interpolating gaps both passes did not make was the main source of bidirectional's early WRONG predictions.

    Limits¶

    • Small, hand-selected ground truth; the held-out set is 119 ball frames.
    • GT frames are mostly ones a person looked at because the detector proposed something; recall is optimistic.
    • Pixel distance only; no check of the ball size or the pitch position.
    • Clips are scored at native resolution (one 1280x720 source, the rest 1920x1080).
    • Whole-match frames in v11 were attributed to clips from the segment time table; a clip could still share footage with training in ways this does not detect.
