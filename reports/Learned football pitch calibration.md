# Anchor with learned keypoints, then track the camera

The best route from our classical pipeline is **not a full replacement but a swap of its weakest stage**. Replace Claude-localised keypoints with an open learned detector, PnLCalib, and keep our RANSAC, distance-transform and k1 refinement. Then add a temporal layer that propagates a physically parameterised camera (pan, tilt, focal length, k1) between anchor frames, re-anchors when line overlap drops, and smooths within shots. PnLCalib is the strongest openly reproducible per-frame stack: GPL-2.0-or-later code, CC BY 4.0 weights, SN23-test JaC@5 of 78.7 with refinement ([PnLCalib](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08401v5); [repo](https://github.com/mguti97/PnLCalib)). The temporal layer matters more on video: on 1080p SoccerNet-GSR sequences, tracking-based BroadTrack reaches 56.9 JaC@5 against 37.1 for per-frame NBJW ([BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721)). Lens distortion (k1), which we already model, is the largest single published gain (42.1 to 54.1 JaC@5). A hard fixed camera centre hurts, so use a soft tripod prior instead. Our human ground truth (4–8 px) is about 0.1–0.25 m near the camera but roughly 1–1.6 m at the far touchline [computed, below]. Evaluation must therefore be in metres by depth, and chasing JaC@2 is pointless. A parallel keyframe-propagation experiment and a zero-shot PnLCalib test should decide how Phase 1 is weighted. This report does not assume their results.

## Open learned detectors exist, but only the PnLCalib family is both strong and adoptable

Almost all strong recent methods share one shape: a CNN predicts keypoints and line extremities (or segments), then a classical solver recovers the camera. Most output a pinhole camera. Distortion is modelled by few: BroadTrack and Theiner 2026 model k1, TVCalib has it optional, and PnLCalib added it in 2026 without published numbers ([PnLCalib repo](https://github.com/mguti97/PnLCalib); [Theiner 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/CVsports/papers/Theiner_Unified_Sports_Field_Registration_with_Lens_Distortion_Modeling_CVPRW_2026_paper.pdf)). Because player feet lie on the ground plane, only the ground homography derived from the camera is needed for projection; the full camera buys physical constraints, distortion and off-plane landmarks.

Two newer systems have no usable code. Theiner 2026 promises source but none is confirmed, and Constructor Tech's 2024 winner and Broadcast2Pitch show no downloadable weights. Both are useful as design references only.

Published numbers must be read with their protocols. On 540p SN22-center, PnLCalib with PnL gets 80.6 JaC@5 against NBJW's 63.9 and TVCalib's 54.8 ([PnLCalib](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08401v5)). On the homography benchmarks, projection error is **0.60 m mean on WC14 and 0.23 m on TS-WorldCup**, but all 2022–2026 methods now cluster tightly on IoU, so those benchmarks no longer discriminate ([PnLCalib](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08401v5)). The comparison that matches our footage is 1080p video. There, NBJW has 37.1 JaC@5 and 10.3 px mean reprojection error, BroadTrack 56.9 and 5.0 px, and Broadcast2Pitch (author-run) 69.4 and 4.5 px ([BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721); [Broadcast2Pitch](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/papers/Oo_Broadcast2Pitch_Game_State_Reconstruction_from_Unconstrained_Soccer_Videos_WACV_2026_paper.pdf)). So a per-frame learned detector alone lands near 10 px on HD video, which is worse than our human ground truth. The gap to about 5 px comes from distortion, refinement and temporal handling, which our pipeline partly has already.

Failure modes will shape our results. Every keypoint method needs about four visible landmarks. It struggles on central close-ups (PnLCalib JaC@5 22.4 there), fisheye in-goal shots, and sub-sequences between the penalty box and halfway line where few segments are visible ([Magera 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.20052); [Theiner 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/CVsports/papers/Theiner_Unified_Sports_Field_Registration_with_Lens_Distortion_Modeling_CVPRW_2026_paper.pdf)). Inference of PnLCalib on the RTX 2070 should be fine. Fine-tuning at batch 1–2 probably fits in 8 GB, but no source states VRAM; the authors trained on a 12 GB card ([PnLCalib](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08401v5)). Test it before relying on it. Inference runs at roughly 2–6 FPS in the authors' timing, so subsample the 25 fps clips and propagate in between.

## About 25k labelled frames are free to download, but none carries a data licence

SoccerNet-Calibration-2023 (about 4 GB on Hugging Face, not gated) holds roughly 22k broadcast frames annotated with line extremities and circle points rather than homographies. It has no licence tag, and the repo has no LICENSE file ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SoccerNet/SN-Calibration-2023); [sn-calibration](https://github.com/SoccerNet/sn-calibration)). Per-split counts conflict across papers (21,132, 22,816 and 14,512 are all quoted), so verify after download. TS-WorldCup adds 3,812 sequence frames from 43 videos (588 MB, [project page](https://ericsujw.github.io/KpSFR)). WorldCup 2014 has 395 images (209 train, 186 test) but imprecise homographies: JaC@5 is 67.4 with the originals, 79.1 with corrected labels and 92.5 for a distortion-aware model ([ProCC](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.09807v1)). Use it only as a stress test. SN-GSR-2025 (35 GB, GPL-3.0 tag) is auxiliary. Spiideo SynLoc has synthetic players on static installation cameras, so only its camera model transfers ([sskit](https://github.com/Spiideo/sskit)). The pixels are third-party broadcast footage. If the project stays internal and research-only, the practical risk is low. Do not redistribute images or weights derived from them without checking, and note that PnLCalib's own base weights inherit that history.

The evidence on how many in-domain labels you need is thin, which is the most important fact for our 50 accepted calibrations. The only direct data point is NBJW fine-tuned on 209 WC14 images: IoU_whole rose from 89.4 to 92.2 and projection error fell from 1.23 m to 0.68 m ([NBJW](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.08401v1)). No paper tests 50 frames. Our 50 accepted frames across 3 matches are therefore the evaluation set first and a fine-tuning set second. Use leave-one-match-out splits so that stadium-specific features cannot leak between train and test.

Synthetic data has a weak evidence base at scale and no evidence at our scale. The Constructor Tech winner trained a 5M-parameter camera regressor on 40,000 Google Research Football renders mixed with 22,000 real frames, then 200k real-only batches, but retrieved text includes no synthetic-versus-real ablation ([Golovkin](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06357v1)). SoccerSynth-Field, the one clean ablation, found Unreal Engine pretraining added just **+0.2 to +0.5 points** of line-segmentation accuracy at 6,000–14,000 real frames ([SoccerSynth-Field](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13969)). I rank synthesis below the other levers. If we do it, a generator that renders our own pitch model through sampled cameras is cheap. A defensible prior blends Chen & Little's measured WC14 ranges (pan ±35°, tilt −15° to −5°, focal 1000–6000 px at 720p) with Golovkin's wider uniform sampling, plus randomised grass, line width, distractor lines, shadows and distortion ([Chen & Little](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.10658)). Line width, motion blur and distortion randomisation have not been studied in any paper I read.

Re-warping our real labelled frames is the cheaper augmentation, and one hockey study measured it. Zoom, flip and colour jitter with the zoom and flip composed into the label homography lifted IoU_whole from 86.4 to 87.8, mostly on zoomed-in frames. Copy-pasting players for occlusion added only +0.1 to +0.2 ([Shang et al.](https://openjournals.uwaterloo.ca/index.php/vsl/article/download/5374/5662/24730)). Jiang et al. also regenerate random perturbations of the true homography each epoch for a refinement net ([Jiang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.08034)). Three pitfalls apply to us (the first two are my inference from the sources). A plane warp misplaces non-planar content such as stands, boards and goals, so restrict virtual camera moves to pan, tilt, roll and zoom about the camera centre. Those are pure-rotation image homographies, exact for the whole scene if the centre is fixed, and they suit a PTZ rig. If labels are a camera model rather than a bare H, re-express intrinsics and k1 after the warp. Horizontal flips must swap left/right classes and mirror the template, since SoccerNet class names encode sides ([sn-calibration](https://github.com/SoccerNet/sn-calibration)).

Pseudo-labelling is how our ~150 Claude proposals and unlabelled video become training signal. There is no peer-reviewed closed loop with a human verifier. The nearest evidence is SoccerMaster, which auto-labelled 2.7M frames from 500 matches with detector plus PnL, unverified by humans ([SoccerMaster](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11016)), and a patent family that sends confident labels to the training set and uncertain ones back to a pool ([USPTO 11816849](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11816849)). From these I infer three verifier signals: agreement between two independent estimators, line-pixel reprojection error, and temporal consistency. Human time should go to the uncertain middle band. Accepted labels will skew toward easy frames, so keep the fixed held-out matches untouched. Rejected proposals are also useful as negatives for the confidence gate.

## Tracking the camera between anchors is where video pays off

A broadcast main camera is a PTZ head on a tripod. The standard model splits the projection into a time-invariant base (centre and base rotation) and per-frame pan, tilt and focal length. With the base known, two correspondences give a per-frame pose, and a RANSAC sample needs 16 iterations against 71 for a four-point homography ([Chen, Zhu & Little](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.09005)). The assumption that the base is fixed was reported to hold generally on two World Cup games. The most recent evidence qualifies it. BroadTrack argues the effective optical centre moves along the axis with zoom on 25x lenses, and its ablation shows forcing a fixed centre costs JaC@5 (50.97 against 56.88 for a soft tripod). Under that soft constraint, a tripod point T and a distance δ stay fixed for the whole game while the focal point slides along the optical axis. NBJW's per-frame focal point wandered up to 20 m inside one 30 s sequence, whereas the tripod kept it near T ([BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721)). Single-frame metrics barely moved from the tripod (54.1 to 54.9). Its benefit is plausible, stable geometry, not headline accuracy. Use the tripod as a soft prior with a per-camera T, and expect main, 16 m and behind-goal cameras to need separate ones.

Between anchors, propagate rather than detect. The reliable design, assembled from BroadTrack, PTZ-SLAM and the 2025 GSR entries, has these parts. Parameterise the state as (pan, tilt, f), not homography entries. Fitting the pan/tilt/f state through an EKF beat a homography EKF on soccer: pan error 0.08° against 0.74° and focal error 64 px against 179 px ([Lu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.08816)). Add a line-reprojection term every frame whenever markings are visible, and a Lucas-Kanade flow term on field-plane points with players masked, to avoid drift where markings are sparse. Monitor the Jaccard overlap between the detected markings and the projected template, and re-anchor from the detector when it drops below 0.5. BroadTrack re-initialised 33 times across 49 sequences, with about 15 frames lost each time. Masking players matters: removing the person detector raised focal error from 63.7 to 110.6 px in PTZ-SLAM, and unmasked ECC was the worst tracker in a 2026 ablation (0.107° against 0.041–0.043° for LK and RAFT-small) ([SMART](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.31551)). Pure frame-chaining without anchoring is not viable: Sharma et al. measured 35% IoU for KLT chaining against 85% for per-frame registration ([Sharma](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.01437)). Sparse LK plus RANSAC on a downscaled grayscale frame is CPU-cheap, so it can run on the Mac mini, while the learned detector stays on the RTX 2070. RAFT-small adds no accuracy over LK in that ablation.

Smoothing should act on camera parameters, because camera error is common-mode across all 22 players and smoothing projected positions cannot remove it. The 2024 GSR winner applied a Savitzky-Golay filter to camera parameters with a 15-frame (0.5 s) delay, bounded to ±2° and ±2 m ([Golovkin](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.06357)). Smooth only within detected shot segments. Cuts average one per ~9 s in SoccerNet-v2, so most frames sit in multi-second main-camera segments, which is the regime where few anchors pay off ([SoccerNet-v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.13367)). A cheap shot-boundary detector (histogram or TransNetV2) plus a main-camera classifier plus the line-overlap gate decides which frames are calibratable; I found no published measurement of that gate. A match-level bundle adjustment with shared {T, δ, principal point, k1} and per-frame {pan, tilt, f} is plausible. Sinha and Pollefeys reached 0.43 px mean reprojection on panoramas with zoom-dependent distortion ([Sinha & Pollefeys](https://snsinha.github.io/pdfs/SinhaCVIU2006.pdf)), and PTZ-Calib runs incremental and global BA on ray landmarks ([PTZ-Calib](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09075)). No paper measures the gain of match-wide BA over per-frame estimation on football, so treat it as an optional later step to be tested on our labels. The shared k1 and focal ambiguity (zoom against distortion) needs priors or fitting on line-rich frames. As for code, BroadTrack is research-only licensed, and PTZ-SLAM reportedly crashes after about 200 frames on SoccerNet footage ([BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01721)). Reimplementing the camera model and per-frame solve with SciPy least-squares on our existing refinement is the sound route.

## Metres by depth, not pixels, decide whether projection is good enough

Pixel metrics hide a 6x depth effect. My own pinhole calculation (1920x1080, camera 18 m high and 12 m behind the near touchline, 55° horizontal FoV; not from a source, and our actual rig will differ) gives these ground errors for a 1 px vertical image error: **0.03 m at 20 m from the near touchline, 0.10 m at the far side of the centre circle, 0.20 m at the far touchline**. Lateral error per pixel is about five times smaller. A 0.1° tilt error gives about 0.66 m at the far touchline, and a 1% focal error about 0.56 m. Our human ground truth of 4–8 px is therefore about 0.1–0.25 m near but about 0.8–1.6 m at the far touchline. That is the noise floor of anything we compare to it. It also means SoccerNet-style 5 px tolerances at 540p, equal to 10 px at 1080p, correspond to roughly 2 m on the far touchline. A systematic tilt or focal error shifts the whole far half of the pitch at once, so it matters more than random keypoint noise. The foot point adds its own bias. If the box bottom sits 0.1–0.3 m above the turf, projected positions move by about 0.15–0.44 m at 20 m from the near touchline and up to **1.36 m at the far touchline** [computed]. Bounding-box quality can therefore rival calibration error.

Targets come from the few validations available. Crang et al. compared three commercial broadcast-tracking providers with TRACAB Gen 5 on a World Cup match. They considered about 1 m position error suitable for spatial analysis and found overall position RMSE of 1.68–16.39 m. When players were detected, RMSE was only 0.44–1.14 m. Extrapolated off-screen frames ran to 4.6–19.4 m, so availability dominates calibration quality in broadcast tracking ([Crang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19477)). Theiner et al. judged 2 m "correct" and under 3 m "potentially useful" for Bundesliga TV tracking, with 55–79% of positions within 2 m ([Theiner et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.11107)). GS-HOTA gives half credit near 2.4 m and zero beyond 5 m ([GSR paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.11335)). I recommend 1 m as the goal at ordinary depths and 2 m as acceptable at the far touchline, with 5 m as the failure threshold. Calibration failure is catastrophic rather than gradual: one GSR sequence scored 0.23% against 49.7% for a good one. So the pipeline must flag and drop low-confidence frames instead of emitting positions. The 2024 GSR baseline also shows calibration is first-order: oracle-calibration rows sit near 50 GS-HOTA against 22.3 for the full baseline.

Evaluate on our 50 accepted frames as follows, split by match. Annotate points on markings and goal posts and back-project them through the camera to the template, reporting error in metres by depth bin alongside JaC@5 and IoU as diagnostics only. Add 200–500 player foot contacts on or beside known markings (penalty spot, box corners, wall lines) so end-to-end foot-point error is measured, because no paper isolates calibration's effect on foot points. Report mean, median, p90, the fraction within 1, 2 and 5 m, and the valid-calibration fraction. Add a stability test: reproject static landmarks through a sequence and measure jitter, as NBJW's wandering focal point would show. Following Crang, split results into detected and extrapolated samples. Because JaC is in pixels, unnormalised, and ignores temporal consistency, it cannot predict metre error on our footage ([Theiner 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/CVsports/papers/Theiner_Unified_Sports_Field_Registration_with_Lens_Distortion_Modeling_CVPRW_2026_paper.pdf)).

## A four-phase plan, with the parallel experiments gating Phase 1

All accuracy figures below are my estimates anchored to the cited numbers, not results from our data, and effort is for one engineer.

| Phase | What | Effort | Expected outcome (estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Metres-by-depth evaluation harness on the 50 accepted frames, leave-one-match-out; wait for the two parallel experiments | 2–3 days | defines our baseline; no accuracy gain |
| 1 | Learned proposer plus existing refinement; or keyframe propagation, per the experiments | about 1 week | removes Claude from the loop for most frames; targets near the 4–8 px human floor on main-camera frames |
| 2 | Temporal layer: shot gate, soft tripod, LK flow with player mask, line-IoU re-anchoring, SG smoothing | 2–3 weeks | stable parameters; low-single-digit px median on tracked segments, far-side metre error under about 1–2 m |
| 3 | Fine-tune PnLCalib heads on our frames, plus augmentation and optional synthesis | 1–2 weeks | fewer re-anchors and fewer rejected frames; size of gain unknown |
| 4 | Self-training loop and optional match-level BA | ongoing | scale to 200 clips and beyond |

Phase 0 is the one thing to do regardless. Build the harness before judging any method, because the choice between propagating from human keyframes and running a learned detector on every frame depends on numbers we do not yet have. The two parallel experiments should set the weights of Phase 1. If zero-shot PnLCalib with its PnL refinement already lands close to accepted calibrations on most main-camera frames, Phase 1 is detector-first: run PnLCalib at 1–5 fps as the proposer, feed its keypoints and lines into our RANSAC and distance-transform refinement with k1, and send only low-confidence frames to Claude. If keyframe propagation holds metre-level accuracy over many seconds of a clip, Phase 1 is propagation-first: spend the effort on the anchor selection and re-anchoring logic of Phase 2, and use the detector only for anchors. If both are strong, do both and compare on the same harness. If neither is, the likely culprit is the far-side, sparse-marking and close-up frames described above, and the plan proceeds to Phase 3 earlier. Phase 1 also needs a cheap confidence score per frame (marking Jaccard against the projected template), since everything downstream depends on gating.

Phase 2 builds the temporal layer described above, reimplemented in Python/SciPy. Fit a per-camera T, δ and k1 once per match on the accepted calibrations (BroadTrack used the first 5,000 frames for one stadium), then freeze them. This is where a few good frames calibrate many, and it is within reach of the Mac mini for the flow and solve steps. The detector stays on the 2070 through Prefect.

Phase 3 fine-tunes only if error analysis points at the detector. Convert each accepted homography to keypoints and line extremities, mask non-planar points as PnLCalib does, use augmentation (zoom/crop through composed homographies, pure-rotation re-warps, flip with side swap, colour and shadow), and mix in SoccerNet frames to avoid forgetting. Use lr 1e-5 with batch 1–2, as the authors did, and confirm 8 GB fit first. Include Claude proposals only after filtering by reprojection consistency, to avoid teaching the model the labeller's biases. Synthetic pretraining stays optional: published gains are small and untested below 6,000 real frames.

## Conclusion

The literature changes the shape of the problem. Our bottleneck is not the solver or the lens model, since we already do refinement and k1. It is the manual keypoint localisation that does not scale and the lack of any temporal structure. The published numbers say a per-frame learned detector alone will land near 10 px on 1080p video, so the win comes from pairing it with refinement and soft-tripod propagation. That combination reached about 5 px mean reprojection in BroadTrack, close to our human floor. Because the human floor is itself about 1 m on the far touchline, the project should stop optimising pixel agreement beyond that point. Effort is better spent on confidence gating and on dropping frames that cannot be calibrated, since extrapolated and failed frames cost far more (4.6–19 m) than any calibration refinement recovers.

Several questions stay open and will need our own data. No paper tests fine-tuning on about 50 frames, measures the metre-level benefit of match-wide bundle adjustment, quantifies the PnLCalib distortion option, or validates a calibratable-frame gate. The leave-one-match-out harness from Phase 0 is the instrument that answers them, which is why it comes before everything else.
