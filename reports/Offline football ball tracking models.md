# Build the solver, fine-tune the heatmaps

**No off-the-shelf model does what the brief asks, so the answer is a hybrid: fine-tune one off-the-shelf heatmap family, build the sequence-level solver, and reuse existing tools for players and camera motion.** Every published multi-frame ball tracker (TrackNet V2-V5, WASB, BlurBall, TOTNet) runs at a fixed 288x512 input, a 3.75x downscale that shrinks our ~11 px (at 1280) ball to roughly 4-5 px, and none has been shown on a tiny ball in a panning, zooming wide broadcast shot ([WASB](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.05237), [TrackNetV3](https://people.cs.nycu.edu.tw/~yushuen/data/TrackNetV3.pdf)). WASB-SBDT (MIT) is the best starting point because it ships soccer weights and keeps a full-resolution HRNet, but its soccer evidence (F1 88.3) comes from a fixed-camera 1080p set and its own causal tracker does not help on soccer. The offline requirements (whole-clip prior, occlusion hold, ball-absent starts) are not provided by any released model, so a bidirectional decoder over candidates must be built. We recommend a candidate-then-sequence design: M0 is a non-learned bidirectional Viterbi over the existing RT-DETR candidates (about a week, CPU, no training). M1 is a native-resolution, camera-motion-warped multi-frame heatmap net initialised from WASB. M2 is a small bidirectional transformer over candidate sets. M3 is audited self-training on unlabelled matches. All of it fits the RTX 2070 8 GB queue, and the labelling effort should switch this week from isolated boxes to dense, state-annotated 10-second windows, starting with a leakage-free Norwich eval set. Five validating experiments are in flight (see the last section); until they report, the model-choice rankings below are evidence-based priors, not measured results.

## No pretrained model fits the brief, but five pieces can be reused

The published landscape splits cleanly into things that detect a tiny ball from a frame stack and things that reason over a trajectory, and our brief needs both at a scale nobody has tested. WASB is the strongest detector candidate: a ~1.5M-parameter HRNet with strides removed so features stay at input resolution, three stacked frames, MIT code, and per-sport weights including soccer, scoring soccer F1 88.3 at a 4 px tolerance against 86.6 for TrackNetV2 ([WASB paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.05237), [model zoo](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nttcom/WASB-SBDT/main/MODEL_ZOO.md)). Three caveats cut the headline. The paper never reports ball pixel size, its soccer set is 4 training clips (11,994 frames) rather than a PTZ broadcast, and the README describes the NTT repo as evaluation code for pretrained models only, so training should go through the BlurBall repo (MIT), which adds training scripts on top of WASB ([BlurBall](https://github.com/cogsys-tuebingen/blurball)). Our own KB is a warning about transfer: off-the-shelf YOLO collapsed to 0.04 AP@0.5 on ISSIA-3D until fine-tuned to 0.65 ([SoccerNet-v3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10106v1)), and resolution, not label volume, was the lever that moved our detector (640 px gave zero ball recall, 1280 px worked) ([KB: footy-ball-detection-findings](file:///home/george/knowledge/wiki/footy-ball-detection-findings.md)).

For gap-filling, TrackNetV3's InpaintNet is the only released piece that matches "hold the ball through occlusion using positions on both sides". It is a 1D-conv U-Net over a coordinate sequence plus a mask, trained with ~30% random masking, and it lifts badminton recall from 95.9% to 99.3% while costing 1.8 points of precision ([TrackNetV3](https://people.cs.nycu.edu.tw/~yushuen/data/TrackNetV3.pdf)). The weights are badminton-specific and the fill rule assumes a fixed court camera, so it is a template to retrain on camera-compensated coordinates, not a download. TOTNet contributes a transferable recipe, masking the ball in the target frame so the net must use neighbours, which cut fully-occluded table-tennis RMSE from 37.3 to 7.2, but its data are research-only ([TOTNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09650v1)). The strongest evidence for our exact regime is YOLOMG: 1080p video, objects mostly under 12x12 px, differences from aligned t-k and t+k frames (homography via LK + RANSAC), and AP@0.5 rising from 0.53 for single-frame YOLOv8 to 0.78 at 640 and 0.85 at 1280 ([YOLOMG](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07115)). It is GPL-3.0 with no weights, so we take the design, not the code. Promptable VOS is the weakest fit: SAM2-family models run at a fixed ~1024 px square with stride-16 memory, so an 11 px ball is about one feature cell, and the only small-fast-object benchmark gives mean mIoU 0.44-0.51 ([FMOX](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09633v1)). SAM2.1 survives only as a crop-based candidate source, and point trackers (CoTracker3, AllTracker) have no ball evidence at all and are better used for camera motion ([CoTracker3](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11831)). Players are the opposite story: GTA-Link (MIT) added 3.7 HOTA on SoccerNet over Deep-EIoU ([GTA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.08216)), so players should use existing tools.

The ranking below orders options by expected payoff per unit of effort for our constraints (8 GB, offline, tiny ball, PTZ).

## A candidate-then-sequence pipeline fits an 8 GB card and stages the risk

An end-to-end long-video transformer is the wrong shape for this hardware. At the stride-8 features the ball needs, one 1920x1080 frame is about 8.3M activations per layer, so a 64-frame clip is ~530M per layer before any attention, which exceeds 8 GB in training, and cropping to fit destroys the long-range context that is the point ([design note, arithmetic](file:///home/george/footy-track/research_notes/Offline%20football%20ball%20tracking%20models/custom_model_design_and_labelling.md)). Splitting the problem into per-frame candidates and a clip-level sequence model keeps the ball at native resolution and the long reach cheap. The measured constraint that shapes everything is that about 37% of held-out ball frames have no RT-DETR candidate within 25 px, so the candidate source is the recall ceiling, and a decoder alone can mostly buy precision and short-gap recall ([KB: autolabel loop](file:///home/george/knowledge/wiki/footy-claude-autolabel-loop.md)). That argues for building the decoder first (cheap, immediately useful as a labelling assistant) and the better candidate source second. Recall (0.21 vs 0.59 for the same weights) is still unresolved in the KB, so E0 re-measures the per-frame baseline before anything is compared to it.

The state model is the part that implements the brief. Each frame is in one of four states: visible, occluded-in-play, out-of-shot, or dead. Human anchors clamp a state, "not ball" verdicts forbid a candidate, and the forward-backward posterior means a confident sighting 40 frames later pulls the path back toward it. A clip that starts with no visible ball is handled by the out-of-shot or occluded state with positions back-extrapolated from the first sighting. Occlusion hold is a growing-radius state bounded by a ~65 px/frame speed limit (the measured human-placed p99 is 63.6 px/frame at 1080p). Camera motion enters as per-frame similarity transforms chained to a clip reference frame, with player boxes and the broadcast overlay masked, and pitch metres can replace those later when the calibration work lands. Player context (distance to the nearest foot point, inside-box, local occupancy) enters as features rather than a joint model, because pretrained player-to-ball transformers such as Ball Radar run on pitch-coordinate tracks at 6.25 Hz and are design references only ([Ball Radar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08206)).

| Milestone | Build | Compute | Success metric (on held-out sets, window-level bootstrap CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E0** (1-2 days) | Eval harness: leave-anchors-out, held-out clips and match; not the causal `BallTracker` protocol | CPU | Re-measures per-frame RT-DETR with instance counts; gates all later claims |
| **M0** (~1 week) | Camera-motion transforms; 4-state HMM/Viterbi over RT-DETR candidates (conf >= 0.1); anchor clamping; piecewise gap fill; tier-3 outputs | CPU, ms/clip | F1@10 px on visible frames beats best-threshold per-frame RT-DETR with a CI excluding 0; report state accuracy; becomes the labelling assistant |
| **M1** (1-2 weeks) | "BallHeat": WASB-initialised HRNet or ResNet-18-FPN, 5 frames warped into frame t, native-res 512x512 crops, quality-focal loss, occlusion augmentation, top-16 peaks | 2070, AMP, batch 8-16 [assumed; measure first] | Candidate recall@16 within 8 px clearly above RT-DETR on the held-out match; end-to-end F1 re-measured through M0 |
| **M2** (1-2 weeks) | "BallSeq": 6-layer bidirectional transformer, d=192, over candidate sets, camera motion, player features and optional anchor tokens; pointer, position, state and confidence heads; trained on real tracks plus synthetic corruption (dropped candidates, injected false ones, 2-40 frame masks) | <1 GB VRAM | Beats M0+M1 on OCC position error and state F1; F1@10 versus anchors per minute (0, 1/50 frames, 1/25) |
| **M3** (ongoing) | Self-training on the 97k Man City frames and unlabelled other matches; "attached to player" mode; pitch metres | Queued GPU jobs | >= 95% of audited pseudo-track frames within 10 px before promotion; Norwich never touched |

Throughput is the open engineering risk. Full-frame BallHeat inference is estimated at >= 10 fps on the 2070 (~3 h for 97k frames) and training memory at batch 8 of 512x512 crops has not been measured; both must be benchmarked before M1 is committed to. Jobs run through the Prefect gpu pool at concurrency 1, which suits this plan because M0 and M2 are CPU/light-GPU and only M1 and M3 inference need the card. A 12-16 GB rental is a fallback only if M1 will not fit, and the end-to-end video model should be revisited only if M2 shows that appearance evidence, not sequence reasoning, is what limits it.

## Dense state-annotated windows replace isolated boxes as the labelling unit

The model needs contiguous, fully accounted-for windows, not more isolated boxes. Today's human-tier GT is 1,515 ball frames over 35 clips, but median run length is 1, only 9 runs reach 25 frames, and with the VitTrack-checked rows removed (human-placed only) there are none ([KB measurements in design note](file:///home/george/footy-track/research_notes/Offline%20football%20ball%20tracking%20models/custom_model_design_and_labelling.md)). Those long runs are also biased: checked-VitTrack tracks move at a median 3.4 px/frame while human-placed consecutive pairs move at 14.4, so new windows should deliberately target fast play. Five times more single-frame labels bought nothing earlier ([KB](file:///home/george/knowledge/wiki/footy-ball-detection-findings.md)), so the marginal label that matters is one that makes absence meaningful. That means a ball centre point on keyframes (a click in a zoomed loupe, with a target precision of ~2 px at 1080p), state spans (visible, occluded, out-of-shot, dead), and a coverage flag asserting that every frame in the span is accounted for. Spare and parked balls are logged as hard negatives because they were the dominant error in Claude-assisted verdicts.

Keyframe spacing comes from our own interpolation test. Linear interpolation between keyframes 5 frames apart lands within 5 px on 90% of skipped frames on the mixed set (84% on the small human-placed-only set, n=56) and within 10 px on 95-98%, while at every 10 frames human-placed accuracy collapses to 36% within 5 px. So the default is a keyframe every 5 frames plus every touch or direction change, going to 10 only for slow rolling ball once model-assisted interpolation exists. Quantity targets are extrapolated from WASB's 4 clips and ~12k frames: about 60-100 windows (15-25k covered frames) across at least 3 matches, which at an assumed 5-8 minutes per window is 7-12 hours of labelling; time the first three windows before trusting that. The sidecar format stays as it is, with new marker tags (`point_only`, `touch`, `uncertain`, `occluded_guess`, `interp`) registered in the labeller and `ingest_gt` to avoid repeating the T-01 marker-tag bug, and spans live in a new append-only `ball_spans/` file. Until that exists, spans go into a plain `ball_spans_manual.csv`.

## Norwich and leave-anchors-out evaluation keep every claim honest

The project has already been burned by a 44-instance validation set (ball mAP50 0.696 against ~0.22 on the broader set), and v11-val's 211 balls are 74% from one 10-second clip, so evaluation design comes before model work ([KB](file:///home/george/knowledge/wiki/footy-ball-detection-findings.md)). Norwich is the only match with zero files in any detector dataset, so it is the held-out match (EV-M: 20 windows from at least 15 distinct clips, both halves), complemented by held-out clips from training matches (EV-C: at least 15 windows with zero frames in any training set). `astonvilla_seg080`, `bournemouth_1st_seg070` and `arsenal_example` are excluded from all eval because the detector has memorised them, and the canonical clip id from the T-03 change enforces the split through an `eval_split.json` manifest. Each set needs at least 500 visible human keyframes from at least 15 windows with no window over 10% of instances, plus at least 50 occluded, 30 out-of-shot and 20 dead spans, and all intervals are window-level bootstrapped because within-window frames are correlated. These counts are our assumption, justified by the binomial interval (about +/-4 points at n=500, widened by correlation), not a literature standard.

The protocol is leave-anchors-out, the honest metric for a non-causal method, since the existing bake-off harness calls `track()` forward-only and a whole-clip method would score near-perfectly "by construction" there ([in-repo research](file:///home/george/footy-track/docs/design/bidirectional_tracking_research.md)). We hide interior keyframes (gaps of 2, 5, 10 and 25 frames) and also hide anchors themselves, give the model only the remaining anchors, and score at human keyframes only: recall at 5 and 10 px (WASB's strict reference is 4 px), precision (predictions on occluded, out-of-shot or dead frames inside coverage count as false positives), four-way state macro-F1, occlusion-hold position error, continuity (fragments and wrong-object switches per minute), and an anchor-efficiency curve of F1@10 against anchors per minute, which measures "I know where the ball is in one frame". The baselines on the same sets are per-frame RT-DETR at its best threshold, M0, M0+BallHeat, BallSeq, and a WASB-pretrained sanity run. The literature offers no standard protocol here; TAP-Vid's query-first and strided protocols and TAPNext++'s re-detection metric are the closest analogues ([TAPNext++](https://tap-next-plus-plus.github.io)).

## Risks and licence flags

The main risks are candidate recall (the decoder cannot find balls nobody proposed), unmeasured VRAM and throughput for native-resolution training, camera-motion estimation on grass-only close-ups (no source validates KLT there, so the residual of player foot tracks after compensation must be measured), overfitting the sequence model to few real tracks (mitigated by synthetic corruption and the held-out match), and label noise from interpolated frames (down-weighted, never written as `labeller`). One shot-cut caveat is that the splitter found 0 interior cuts in 5 clips from one match, so cut rates for other broadcasters are unknown, and the transform stack falls back to duplicating frame t on a warp-residual spike. Recall from rectification also carries a precision cost (InpaintNet lost 1.8 points of precision), which matters if occluded positions are unlabelled. A methodological risk is that the KB's two conflicting ball-mAP figures for the current detector (0.221 vs 0.665) are unresolved, so no improvement over "the baseline" should be claimed before E0.

## Conclusion

The decision the research changes is not "off-the-shelf or build" but where the boundary sits. Nothing published handles our ball size, camera and offline requirements, yet the expensive part to build is small: a candidate decoder with explicit visibility states is days of CPU work, learns nothing it cannot already measure from our own verdict bands, and immediately pays back as a labelling assistant. The learned parts (a WASB-initialised heatmap net and a sequence transformer) are justified only after that baseline exists, because the decoder cannot exceed the candidate ceiling and tells us exactly how much headroom a better candidate source must buy.

The unusual insight is that the labels, not the model, are the real bottleneck, and they bind in a specific way: absence needs labelling too. Dense windows with state and coverage spans turn the same hours of effort into negatives, sequences, eval instances and anchor-efficiency curves at once, and a Norwich eval set built before any model comparison is the only thing that will make the results in `docs/experiments/` trustworthy. If the five running experiments contradict a ranking above, the ordering should change, but the labelling plan should not.
